Help

              

                What RapidCache does, and what your download speed is telling you.
              

            

          


          

            
What RapidCache is

            

              Think of your pod's internet connection as a water pipe. Everything you
              download comes through it.
            

            

              Model files normally come from Hugging Face, and Hugging Face lets your pod
              open one tap on that pipe. That is their rule, not our choice.
            

            

              RapidCache keeps the same files, identical files with identical checksums, on
              storage that lets you open sixteen taps at once.
            

            

              Why that matters when things are busy. Your pod shares a
              server with other people's pods. When one of those pods starts pulling a
              large dataset, there is less of the server's connection left for everyone
              else on it, including you. When it finishes, yours comes back.
            

            

              Sixteen weak taps still fill the bucket about sixteen times faster than one
              weak tap.
            


            
What a pod actually is

            

              When you rent a pod, what you are guaranteed is the GPU. The internet
              connection and the storage hardware belong to the machine it sits in, and
              every other pod on that machine is using them too. You cannot see those pods,
              and neither can we.
            

            

              So a good pod and a bad pod are not different products. They are the same pod
              on a quiet server or a busy one, and that can change in minutes.
            


            
Why is my download only 80 MB/s?

            

              
              
            

            
That number is your pod, not the service.

            

              Those are two pods pulling the same 11.46 GB file at the same moment. Both
              were busy. One was signed in to RapidCache, one was not.
            

            

              

                
                  
tapsspeedthat one file

                
                
                  

                    RapidCache16
                    97.2 MB/sabout 2 minutes
                  

                  

                    Standard1
                    6.2 MB/sabout 31 minutes
                  

                
              

            

            

              So 97 MB/s is not RapidCache being slow. It is RapidCache doing its job on a
              machine that only had about 6 MB/s to give a single connection.
            

            

              Nothing is broken when this happens. Every connection is
              still open and still working, so you see no error, only smaller numbers. They
              grow again when the pod that was busy stops.
            


            
What good and bad look like

            

              
              
              
              
            

            
Those four readings are all real installs. From our own measurements:

badly congestedunder 150 MB/s

                
              

            

            

              We have watched one file move 306 to 49 to 233 MB/s in a few
              minutes, with nothing changing at either end. One reading tells you
              nothing.
            


            
When to wait, and when to start a new pod

            
    
              
  • 
                A slow reading in the first minute or two: ignore it.
                Connections take a few seconds to reach full speed, and a 300 MB file
                finishes before they get there. Small files always read lower than big ones.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Still under 150 MB/s after several minutes, with no sign of
                recovery: you are on a busy machine. Stop the pod and deploy a new
                one. You will land on a different machine.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Do not restart immediately. You will usually lose more time
                redeploying than you would have lost waiting, and cancelling to wait costs
                you nothing. We have seen pods recover fivefold on their own.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Not sure? Press Debug. It tells you what
                your pod actually did.
              
    • 
            


            
Cancelling, and picking up where you left off

            

              Cancel stops the install. It does not throw away what has already come down.
            

            

              It takes effect immediately, including while custom nodes are installing,
              which is where an install otherwise sits for minutes.
            

            

              Press the same workflow again on the same pod and it carries on. Files that
              finished are checked rather than downloaded again, and a file that was part
              way through resumes from where it stopped. Nothing is fetched twice.
            

            
Two things worth knowing:

            
    
              
  • 
                This holds on the same pod. Deploy a new one and the
                part-finished file is gone with the old pod's temporary storage. Models that
                already finished survive only if /workspace is a Network
                volume.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                A part-finished file is kept on the pod and cleared automatically after a
                day.
              
    • 
            

            

              So if the server is busy, cancelling costs you nothing. Wait a few minutes and
              press the tile again.
            


            
What the numbers mean

            

              
              
            

            
An install does two different things, and both show a speed:

            
    
              
  • 
                Downloading: pulling the file from the internet. This is
                where your pod's connection matters.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Saving to disk: copying the finished file onto your pod's
                storage. No internet is involved, so it usually reads much higher.
              
    • 
            

            

              Seeing 77 MB/s and then 1.10 GB/s a moment later is normal. They are not the
              same thing.
            


            
Setting up your pod

            
            

              Container disk. Temporary space, wiped when the pod stops.
              Downloads are staged here first. The largest single model is
              28.43 GB, so give it 100 GB or more. Our
              template ships 200 GB.
            

            
Storage for your models. RunPod offers two:

            

              

                
                  

                    Volume disk
                    local to your pod
                    Fastest. Deleted when the pod is terminated.
                  

                  

                    Network volume
                    shared storage
                    
                      Survives termination, mountable by several pods, cheaper. About ten
                      times slower to write.
                    
                  

                
              

            

            

              Size. Dataset Generator is 44.6 GB and MiniMax H3 is 63.4 GB,
              plus ComfyUI itself. 200 GB is comfortable for two or three
              workflows. 100 GB is tight.
            

            

              Environment variables. RunPod adds
              PUBLIC_KEY and JUPYTER_PASSWORD to every pod
              automatically, for its own SSH and Jupyter. RapidCache needs none. Sign in on
              the RapidCache tab instead.
            


            
Signing in

            

              
              
            

            

              Open the RapidCache tab, enter the email and password from
              your subscription, and press Sign in. The green RapidCache
              badge at the top of the workflow list is the only confirmation you need.
            


            
What success looks like

            
            

              100%, and Workflow ready. If it says a number of skipped
              items, open the warning. The install finished, and one optional step did not.
              It is usually safe to continue.
            


            
The Debug button

            
            

              If something looks wrong, press Debug. It writes a report of
              what your pod actually did: how fast each file downloaded, how long each step
              took, what kind of storage you are on, and anything that failed.
            

            

              It carries no passwords, no links and no file locations, so it is safe to
              paste anywhere. Sending it beats describing the problem.
            


            
What we promise

            
RapidCache cannot make your server less busy. Nobody can.

            

              What it does is take as much of whatever your pod has available as the
              connection allows.
            

            

              That is the promise. Not a fixed speed, because nobody can honestly promise a
              speed on shared hardware. The best speed your pod can give you, every time you
              install.
